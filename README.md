# reviewer-eye

**Auditing your own academic draft with a reviewer's eyes — 50 checkpoints, all earned from real audits.**

Every item in this skill came from a moment when a real human challenged a real draft:
a title that promised something the paper never delivered, a sample that did not exist
in the research setting, a word count that did not add up, a direction that had been
polished to a shine on the wrong foundation.

It is not a style guide. It is a list of the ways drafts get rejected — minus the
fabrication and misconduct categories, which need no checklist.

## What problem it solves

Between "correct" and "good enough to submit" there is an entire spectrum of deductions.
Most of them are invisible while you are writing and obvious to a reviewer in five
minutes. This skill moves the reviewer's five minutes to before you hit submit.

## What is inside

Ten sections, 50 numbered checkpoints:

| Section | Concern |
|---|---|
| **A. Structure and reading order** | Document order = reader order; cross-reference sweeps; numbering that survives repeated edits |
| **B. Completeness and promise-keeping** | Candidate pools filled to the list level; promises matched by content; no analysis that stayed in your head |
| **C. Method and evidence** | Method-data mismatch, method-question mismatch, over-claiming, reliability, construct boundaries, method-literature as a mirror of the method stack |
| **D. Ethics and compliance** | IP order (patent before publication), pseudonyms, confidentiality as a channel choice |
| **E. Prepare to the highest standard** | Journal facts verified up front; reverse-calibration **of formatting only**; scope eligibility outranks formatting; indexing tiers not blurred |
| **F. Design feasibility** | Samples must really exist; every content word of the title needs a research question; every number table must be summed and bounded |
| **G. Re-derivation after a change** | Title or journal change invalidates contributions wholesale; variable-type changes propagate through the whole chain |
| **H. Rewriting and expression** | The decision trace before a rewrite; self-coined terminology banned; jargon scrubbed everywhere |
| **I. Journal versus design** | When they conflict, change the journal — the highest-priority item in the skill |
| **J. Direction review** | After three revision rounds, re-examine the direction; never defend the status quo because of sunk cost |

## The two items that save the most work

- **I47 — the journal can change, the proposition cannot.** If satisfying a journal's
  scope requirement would shift the subject of your core proposition, change the journal.
  A journal is a submission channel, not the legislature of your research direction.
- **F39 — every content word of the title must have a research question carrying it.**
  A title promising "evolution" while no research question addresses evolution is an
  empty promise, and it is the first thing a reviewer will ask about.

## Install

Drop the folder into your skills directory:

```
~/.workbuddy/skills/reviewer-eye/
```

Or install from SkillHub (Chinese edition).

## Usage

Load the skill before delivering any academic document, or when any of these happen:

- rewriting a version, reordering sections, expanding a candidate pool
- the title or targeting changes
- the target journal is fixed
- journal requirements appear to conflict with your design
- three or more revision rounds have accumulated
- you are merging several documents into one

Then tick the 50 items. Ship only when all are green.
